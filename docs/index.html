Headline A/B Testing Lab

An end-to-end analysis of 1,825 real headline experiments from the Upworthy Research Archive. It checks the randomization, calibrates the p-values with A/A tests, corrects for multiple comparisons, and shrinks winning lifts toward realistic sizes. Every number on this page is computed by the project's tested Python library.

Analyze your own A/B test

Enter clicks and impressions per arm, with the control first. You get Wilson intervals, z-tests with Holm correction, P(best) and expected loss, a sample-ratio check, and a ship/keep decision. The math runs in your browser and is parity-tested against the Python package.

Plan a test that can actually find something

Most real tests here could only detect lifts of roughly +77% or more. Use this planner to size a test for the effect you actually expect.

Peeking simulator

Simulate A/A tests, where both arms are identical, and "check the dashboard" after every batch of impressions. Stopping at the first significant result turns 5% false winners into far more, and a Bayesian "95% chance to win" stopping rule is no safer.

What makes a headline win?

The features were explored on 1,825 tests, pre-registered, and then confirmed on 8,794 tests the analysis had not seen. Effects are compared within each test, holding the story, image and audience fixed.

Confirmatory-sample estimates with 95% cluster-robust intervals. A filled dot means the feature was pre-registered and replicated. A hollow dot means it was not pre-registered.
